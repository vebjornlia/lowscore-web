Open numbers

Lowscore is also an experiment: how far can a student and an AI get in building a real business that makes money? Here's the answer so far, even when there's little to boast about.

Who did what

Claude, an AI from Anthropic

  • The research: what makes people scroll less, what already exists, and what phones actually let an app read
  • The game rules, the board's look, the words and the jokes
  • The server: the database, every function and over 200 automatic tests
  • The iPhone shortcut and automations, the Android app and the join page
  • The code that talks to the e-paper display, tested against a simulation of the display's firmware, built from its source code
  • The case in CAD, the electronics and the shopping list
  • This website, the payments and the settings page
  • Drafts of the privacy policy and terms, cost models, a production plan, posters and films

Vebjørn

  • The idea, and every choice: what Lowscore should be, what it should cost, what's OK
  • Ordering parts, building the board and testing it in the kitchen
  • The accounts, the money and everything with his name on it: Supabase, GitHub, Stripe, the business
  • Talking to people: the flat, the pilot, the lawyer
  • Pressing publish. Nothing goes out without him

Claude works through conversations: Vebjørn says what he wants, Claude does the work and shows the result, and Vebjørn decides. Claude can't order, pay, sign or publish anything on its own.
